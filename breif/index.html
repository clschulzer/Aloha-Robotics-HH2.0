How a parked C-arm gets its update

Six architectures, one shared brain, and a simulator that runs every option once on the same synthetic hospital. It answers Christian's ask: "spin out architecture with a briefing and diagrams for each and give pros and cons · one shot each solution."

Recommendation: F · one brain, many hands. The brain finds the window and the one person who can say yes. The hand brings power and the package: a person at 17:00, a small data companion on every C-arm, and KettyBot where nobody can power the window. In our synthetic model, stage 1 patches 91 % by the deadline for about €7k of hardware, and stage 2 reaches 92 % and holds up in every scenario we tried. The pilot starts with no robot.

    What changes the answer

    The physics that decides C and D

    A Cios Select data sheet lists 713 W standby at 230 V. A full install on battery is 713 W × 2.5 h ≈ 1.8 kWh, about 2.1 kWh from the pack, which is roughly 18–22 kg of LiFePO4. KettyBot carries 30 kg spread over three 10-kg trays. One robot trip means one install.

    If Siemens has a service mode that powers only the workstation and the flashed subsystems, or if installs shrink to ~30 min, the battery falls to well under 1 kWh. Ask Siemens first: what does an update draw?

    Buy the brain first, then let its data decide which hands to buy.

    Stage 0 · pilot

    Brain + people

    1 hospital · 4–6 C-arms · 8 weeks · no robot needed. Christian's three asks: SRS status data, a C-arm to test, a pilot.

    Hardware €0

    Stage 1

    + data companion

    Package and logs always on board, plus a display that guards the install. Siemens' own favourite idea, in its cheap form.

    ≈ €7k for 12 C-arms · 91 % in the model

    Stage 2

    + one KettyBot

    Only for the C-arms the pilot shows are never powered in time: nights, no socket, thin staff.

    ≈ €49k · 92 %, robust in every scenario

    Later

    Bays, built-in

    Bays in renovations, built-in hands in Siemens' next generation. Same brain, same hand contract.

    Ask Siemens first

    1. What does a Cios draw during an update? Is there a service mode with only the workstation and the flashed subsystems powered?
    2. Can the C-arm switch on without a person, for example when mains returns, by Wake-on-LAN, or by an SRS command? In the model, "nobody to switch it on" is the top lost-window reason for bays, companions and robots.
    3. Can a package be handed over point-to-point on the LAN port from a local mirror (our cache) instead of from SRS directly?
    4. Would you release an isolated battery inverter as the C-arm's mains source at the parking position, never with a patient (IEC 60601-1)?
    5. Are A/B slots or install-while-off on the roadmap? At ~30 min, people plus data covers almost everything.
