# Design review — Ember

This first version follows the user's proposed stage argument: the original film, a brief introduction, the character showcase, an abrupt change of mood, the calendar, the service workflow and an outcome for patients.

OPUS supplied the strongest continuity lesson. The movie's explosion should remain alive when the video ends, becoming the true-story joke and then the title. This version copies those particle and audio primitives and shortens the title landing. It removes the repeated shine and gives each character a large ivory portrait with one state.

The serious half begins with an upward dark transition and “Now.” The theatre board is recognizable before the scouts arrive. A cancellation creates a possible gap; the team's planner selects an eligible result. The scouts provide a visual search metaphor, while the result comes from the model. The later Constellation and Care Loop versions go further by evaluating candidate records on individual scout arrivals.

The architecture grows from the selected window into a small service loop. Paths terminate at icon centres and distinguish the human decision from the proposed physical helper. The final two lines move attention from the equipment to the patient.

The first visual review caught incorrectly initialized canvas dimensions and crowded workflow inputs. Those were corrected, line endpoints were aligned, and the presentation clock was separated from the bounded animation timestep. The final full rehearsal then passed.

This cut is useful when the team wants the most direct character → calendar → architecture structure. Christian's complete phone-to-thank-you product journey is developed separately in versions 02 and 07.
