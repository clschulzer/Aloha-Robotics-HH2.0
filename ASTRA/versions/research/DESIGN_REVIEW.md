# Seven versions: editorial and motion review

## What OPUS gets right

Reviewed `OPUS` at `6a58092`, including its film, ember renderer, audio synthesis, character choreography and scheduler/architecture code. Its strongest feature is continuity: the explosion does not end at the video boundary. Sparks carry the movie into live typography. The joke belongs to the same visual world as the title, so the audience experiences one opening rather than a clip followed by a deck.

The animation works because different materials behave differently. Embers drift and gather; typography locks; characters land with weight; a CRT collapses into a point. Carefully timed sound gives those events a physical edge. Local synthesized sound also avoids unreliable network playback.

What needs changing for this brief: reduce the time between the punchline and a legible title; remove a second title flash; show one character and one state at a time; reserve visual complexity for the animation rather than the labels; replace ornamental architecture branches with a small number of meaningful connections.

## What ASTRA gets right

The original ASTRA sequence has a comprehensible argument: affection → responsibility → the right time → action → care. Its large character showcase, upward dark “Now.” transition, calendar-first explanation and patient-facing ending are worth preserving. The weakness is that its motion sometimes looks like a diagram being demonstrated rather than a designed performance. The new versions must give every movement a cause, destination and finish.

## What Talos teaches

Reviewed the public `ibrahimify/talos` pitch HTML and CSS. A large word can carry a transition if the margins, timing and typography are disciplined. Empty space is useful stage direction. A progressive reveal can explain architecture with less text than a static complete diagram. We use those principles, not a Talos reskin.

## Christian's new material

`CARMAGOTCHI_Web_Pitch/pitch-demo-christian/docs/storyboard-pitch-v4.md` makes the emotional connection operational: mood indicates need and priority; clinical data determines timing. This is a different presentation argument from “show scheduler, then architecture.” Versions 2 and 7 therefore follow that existing product template and role sequence. They must show the actual team demo, not replace it with an unrelated imagined dashboard.

## Grounding

The comprehensive [research recap](../../RESEARCH_RECAP.md) remains the evidence base. The working deliverable is an interactive prototype with real deterministic planning functions and synthetic data. Live hospital interfaces, a physical cable dock, validated emergency prediction and autonomous update execution are not demonstrated by this repository. Architecture proposals remain proposals.

No made-up savings, clinical success rates or AI confidence percentages appear as achieved results. The six pitch cuts preserve the full original film and original characters. Open Circuit is the requested technical presentation and spends its entire duration on engineering. Hardware calibration remains supervised. A selected window is a proposal, not authorization.

The browser evidence lives with each version. Sound is optional after the film. The main film retains its original sound.

## What changed across the seven builds

The first version established the pace and readable stage. The second rendered Christian’s actual product journey. The third experimented with an evening setting and a single golden handoff. The fourth increased the animation and connected the swarm to the actual scorer, including a changed result after an emergency. The fifth made the machine’s personality the comedy engine. The sixth put the executable rules, authorization boundary and proposed stack on stage.

The final version returns to Christian’s template with those lessons. It removes competing text around the phone, shows only each mission phase’s active checks, and carries a calculated cancellation result into the job card and report. The source and all intermediate versions remain intact.

One consequential performance finding: a persistent perspective transform on the large calendar layer substantially reduced Firefox’s frame rate. Removing it after the entrance restored smooth playback. More animation did not require more simultaneous compositing.

The final cut’s [detailed review](../07-encore/research/REVIEW.md) explains the source/template decision and the evidence corrections. The [comparison guide](../README.md) gives a suggested order for rehearsal.

The final delivery pass also supplied a generic monospace fallback for the six cinematic intro captions. Without the named font installed, canvas had used a serif default. All six introductions were captured again through the settled title; this typography correction does not change their cue timing.
